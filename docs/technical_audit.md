## Scientific decisions fixed before new test evaluation

- Prediction is at the end of the index encounter. Admission-time prediction would make hospitalization totals and discharge information unavailable; no such claim is made.
- The smallest eligible encounter identifier is a deterministic selection proxy, not a verified timestamp. The cohort excludes discharge codes 11, 13, 14, 19, 20 and 21; this is a study-population restriction, not proof that every excluded patient's readmission is impossible.
- The prior portfolio holdout has already been inspected. Reusing it preserves comparability but cannot create a pristine confirmatory or external evaluation. New algorithms are developed without adapting to its results.
- Seven configurations per algorithm (initial + six seeded random candidates), three training CV folds, AP selection, one common validation-F1 threshold rule. No early stopping on test or validation data.
- Sigmoid calibration repeats the whole parameter search inside each training calibration fold. It does not reuse the calibration fold's labels for parameter selection.
- Weighting, rare-category pooling and discharge-variable omission are single-factor development sensitivities. They are not promoted after inspecting test scores.
- No ordinary SMOTE on one-hot clinical categories, fabricated cost savings, new repository-wide license, or claims of clinical validation.
